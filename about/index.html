The arc, in plain English

From backend pipes to privacy-first agents.

My career started where most don't admit it does — in the unsexy plumbing. Backend services, distributed systems, the data layer underneath the data layer. The kind of work that doesn't make the keynote but quietly determines whether anything on stage will keep running on Monday morning.

That foundation became the launchpad. As machine learning moved out of research and into shipped product, I followed the wave — first classical ML pipelines, then retrieval-augmented systems, then fine-tuned open-weights models, then the agentic systems we're now arguing about whether to trust. Each step taught me the same lesson from a different angle: the model is the easy part. Everything around it is where products live or die.

Somewhere along that path I realized the field needed a working map. Not another tutorial that ends at "and now you have an LLM app." A field guide that takes a reader from gradient descent all the way to multi-agent orchestration without losing the thread, and treats the parts that hurt — evals, MLOps, safety, privacy — as first-class citizens rather than appendices. That guide became Machine Learning to Gen AI Agents: eighteen chapters, a 177-term glossary, and roughly a year of compressed disagreement with everyone who told me a particular chapter wasn't necessary.

Writing the book made the gap obvious. The privacy chapter described tools that mostly didn't exist outside research papers. So I started building them. That's what Avarna Privacy Gateway is: a POC for the privacy layer the book argued every AI product should have from day one. Three detection layers running in parallel — regex, Microsoft Presidio, and an LLM classifier — masking PII before prompts leave your perimeter, holding the cleartext-to-token map in an encrypted vault, and unmasking the response on the way back. Sub-second round-trip on consumer GPUs. 97% recall in benchmarks. Self-hostable. Model-agnostic. Working today; not yet a company.

Avarna is the most mature of the prototypes on this site, but it isn't the only one. A stock predictor is exploring whether modern feature representations can find signal above the noise floor of public markets. A multi-agent platform is taking shape as a substrate for planning, tool-use, and clean inter-agent handoffs — the kind of orchestration the field still mostly improvises. None of these have customers. All of them have running code.
